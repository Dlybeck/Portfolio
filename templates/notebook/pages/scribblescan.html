ScribbleScan started as my 2025 capstone and grew into a hosted handwriting-digitization service run through my own LLC. It turned handwritten pages into editable documents while keeping images and other non-text elements in place. The hosted service is retired, and the site remains as a demo.

How it grew

  • January 2025 · proposal

    The capstone proposal I started from. Some ideas, like the export options, carried through to the end; others, like the target audience and parts of the feature set, changed along the way.

    {{ links([("Read the proposal", "/static/PDF/ScribbleScan_Proposal.pdf")]) }}
  • February 7th 2025{{ figure("scribblescan/feb7.webp", "First working version of ScribbleScan", cls="shot") }}

    The first working version. It split the results into sections and showed them line by line. It was rough, but it matched the design I had planned.

  • February 8th 2025{{ figure("scribblescan/feb8.webp", "ScribbleScan a day later with larger tiles", cls="shot") }}

    One day later, with major interface improvements: much larger tiles, and a digitize button that hides once pressed. Both carried into every later version.

  • March 12th 2025{{ figure("scribblescan/mar12.webp", "ScribbleScan with export and corrections", cls="shot") }}

    A month after the proof of concept: PDF export, copy to clipboard, corrections and accuracy scores. The focus shifted from a demo to a usable tool.

  • April 28th 2025{{ figure("scribblescan/apr28.webp", "ScribbleScan as presented for the capstone", cls="shot") }}

    The version I presented for my capstone, adding a per-batch page limit, Google sign-in and a cleaner interface. Not yet a full product, but a finished tool.

  • June 25th 2025{{ figure("scribblescan/jun25.webp", "ScribbleScan as a hosted product", cls="shot") }}

    After graduating, I worked on ScribbleScan LLC full time, adding user profiles, support, a cloud-hosted database, paid tiers and many smaller features. It was now a complete product.

  • July 1st 2025{{ figure("scribblescan/jul1.webp", "ScribbleScan with demo mode", cls="shot") }}

    A week later, I considered it finished. The final addition was a demo mode that let anyone try pre-processed samples of difficult handwriting at no cost to the service.
